# Academic Research Proposal: Targeted Physics-Grounded Refinements to `ANCHOR_3OH_AI_PROMPT`

## 1. Root-Cause Physics & Mass-Spectrometric Analysis of the Four Remaining False-Positive Traps

The anchor prompt (`ANCHOR_3OH_AI_PROMPT`) already achieves **92.68% 3-OH recall** (38/41 TP) and a **0.0% false-positive rate on 3-Cl** (0/45 FP), demonstrating that its core structure, line-position table, and Step 4 chloride/hydrate rules are exceptionally well-calibrated. The remaining 25 false positives (which limit precision to 60.32%) stem from four specific physical and instrumental degeneracies in low-resolution ($m/\Delta m \approx 10\text{--}30$) log-amplified CDA time-of-flight (TOF) spectra ($t = t_0 + a\sqrt{m/z}$, $a = 0.473\text{ }\mu\text{s}$):

### Trap A: Type 1 Water-Ice $19\text{ u} \rightarrow 55\text{ u}$ Spacing Degeneracy with $23\text{ u} \rightarrow 63\text{ u}$ (3 FPs)
- **Physical Root Cause:** In pure water-ice spectra (Type 1; Postberg et al. 2008, 2009), the dominant primary ion is $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ at $19\text{ u}$, which arrives at $1.69\text{--}2.09\text{ }\mu\text{s}$—overlapping the lower end of the expected $\text{Na}^+$ ($23\text{ u}$) window ($1.9\text{--}2.3\text{ }\mu\text{s}$) due to $t_0$ jitter. Crucially, the time difference between $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ ($19\text{ u}$) and the second-order water cluster $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{H}_2\text{O})_2$ ($55\text{ u}$) is:
  $$\Delta t_{19 \rightarrow 55} = 0.473 \times (\sqrt{55} - \sqrt{19}) = 1.446\text{ }\mu\text{s}$$
  This is within $0.04\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ (well inside the $\pm 0.10\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ timing uncertainty) of the defining hydroxide spacing between $\text{Na}^+$ ($23\text{ u}$) and $\text{Na}^+(\text{NaOH})$ ($63\text{ u}$):
  $$\Delta t_{23 \rightarrow 63} = 0.473 \times (\sqrt{63} - \sqrt{23}) = 1.486\text{ }\mu\text{s}$$
- **Diagnostic Discriminator:** When the model misidentifies $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ ($19\text{ u}$) as $\text{Na}^+$ ($23\text{ u}$), $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{H}_2\text{O})_2$ ($55\text{ u}$) sits right at $+1.45\text{ }\mu\text{s}$, mimicking $\text{Na}^+(\text{NaOH})$ ($63\text{ u}$). However, a true water-cluster series $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{H}_2\text{O})_n$ always contains the $n=1$ cluster $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{H}_2\text{O})$ at $37\text{ u}$ ($\Delta t_{19 \rightarrow 37} = +0.82\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after the first peak, which is stronger than or comparable to the $+1.45\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ peak) and the $n=3$ cluster $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{H}_2\text{O})_3$ at $73\text{ u}$ ($\Delta t_{19 \rightarrow 73} = +1.98\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $19\text{ u}$, or $+0.53\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $55\text{ u}$), followed by $91\text{ u}$ ($+2.45\text{ }\mu\text{s}$). In contrast, a true 3-OH spectrum has a deep trough or only a weak $\text{K}^+/\text{Na}^+(\text{H}_2\text{O})$ bump at $+0.69\text{--}0.76\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ that is much weaker than the $+1.49\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ $\text{Na}^+(\text{NaOH})$ peak.

### Trap B: 3-Car Carbonate Sensitivity on the Log-Amplified Scale (10 FPs — Primary FP Source)
- **Physical Root Cause:** In carbonate-bearing salt grains (subtype 3-Car; Postberg et al. 2009, 2026), $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ and $\text{NaHCO}_3$ yield the diagnostic cluster $\text{Na}^+(\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3)$ at $129\text{ u}$ ($\Delta t = +3.10\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$) and often the oxide fragment $\text{Na}^+(\text{Na}_2\text{O})$ at $85\text{ u}$ ($\Delta t = +2.09\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$). Because $129\text{ u}$ is a heavier cluster, its log-scaled height on the normalized $0\text{--}1$ plot is often moderate ($0.35\text{--}0.55$), rising $\ge 0.12\text{--}0.15$ units above the local baseline around $+3.05\text{ to }+3.20\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$. The model frequently dismissed this moderate $129\text{ u}$ peak as baseline fluctuations or overlooked the accompanying $85\text{ u}$ peak ($+2.05\text{ to }+2.15\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$).
- **Diagnostic Discriminator:** In Step 5, the prompt must explicitly instruct the scientist that on the log-amplified scale, *even a modest coherent peak* rising $\ge 0.10\text{--}0.15$ above the local baseline at $+3.00\text{ to }+3.20\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$ ($125\text{ u}$ phosphate or $129\text{ u}$ carbonate)—especially when paired with any distinct peak or shoulder at $+2.00\text{ to }+2.15\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$ ($85\text{ u}$ $\text{Na}^+(\text{Na}_2\text{O})$, unless excused by the Step 4a hydrate rule)—is a definitive `non-3-OH` (3-Car / 3-P) signature and must not be dismissed as noise.

### Trap C: 3-KNa / 3-K Potassium Prominence and $\text{K}^+(\text{KOH})$ vs. $\text{Na}^+(\text{NaOH})_2$ Confusion (7 FPs)
- **Physical Root Cause:** In normal sodium-rich salt spectra, $\text{K}^+$ ($39\text{ u}$, $+0.69\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$) is $100\text{--}200\times$ weaker than $\text{Na}^+$ (Postberg et al. 2009). In potassium-enriched grains (3-KNa and 3-K; $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+ < 10$), $\text{K}^+$ at $+0.69\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ does not necessarily equal $\text{Na}^+$ in height; on the log scale, $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+ < 10$ corresponds to a $\text{K}^+$ peak reaching $\ge 0.60\text{--}0.65$ of the $\text{Na}^+$ height (or rising sharply $\ge 0.25\text{--}0.30$ above the post-$\text{Na}^+$ baseline trough as a prominent, sharp second peak before $63\text{ u}$). Furthermore, $\text{K}^+(\text{KOH})$ at $95\text{ u}$ arrives at $\Delta t = +2.34\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$, which the model confused with $\text{Na}^+(\text{NaOH})_2$ at $103\text{ u}$ ($\Delta t = +2.53\text{ }\mu\text{s}$).
- **Diagnostic Discriminator:** Tighten Step 6 so that instead of requiring $\text{K}^+$ to rise "nearly as high as $\text{Na}^+$", a sharp $\text{K}^+$ peak at $+0.65\text{ to }+0.75\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ after $\text{Na}^+$ that exceeds $0.60$ of the $\text{Na}^+$ height (or rises $\ge 0.25$ above the local trough and approaches the height of the $63\text{ u}$ peak without a corresponding hydrate series) is flagged as potassium-rich (`non-3-OH`), and explicitly distinguish $+2.34\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ ($\text{K}^+(\text{KOH})$, $95\text{ u}$) and $+2.76\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ ($\text{K}^+(\text{KCl})$, $113\text{ u}$) from $+2.53\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ ($\text{Na}^+(\text{NaOH})_2$, $103\text{ u}$).

### Trap D: Type 2 Organic-Bearing Water Ice Broad Features vs. Sharp Salt Peaks (5 FPs)
- **Physical Root Cause:** Type 2 spectra (Postberg et al. 2008) contain $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ ($19\text{ u}$) or light organic cations ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_3^+ / \text{CH}_3\text{O}^+$ at $27\text{--}31\text{ u}$) alongside a broad organic/water bulge around $65\text{--}73\text{ u}$ ($3.4\text{--}3.7\text{ }\mu\text{s}$) and slow baseline oscillations in the second half of the record ($3\text{--}6\text{ }\mu\text{s}$).
- **Diagnostic Discriminator:** Reinforce in Step 2, Step 3, and LOOK-ALIKES that true 3-OH peaks ($\text{Na}^+$ at $23\text{ u}$ and $\text{Na}^+(\text{NaOH})$ at $63\text{ u}$) are narrow, distinct mass lines, whereas broad, rounded humps spanning $\ge 0.3\text{--}0.4\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ at the base, clusters of organic peaks between $\text{Na}^+$ and $63\text{ u}$ ($26\text{--}45\text{ u}$), or wavy baseline oscillations in the second half of the trace indicate Type 2 organic-bearing ice (`non-3-OH`).

## 2. Surgical Refinements to `ANCHOR_3OH_AI_PROMPT`

To preserve the anchor prompt's 92.7% recall and 0% 3-Cl FP rate, we keep the entire `ANCHOR_3OH_AI_PROMPT` structure, literature summary, and line-position table intact, and apply **surgical additions** to Steps 2, 3, 5, 6, and LOOK-ALIKES:

### Surgical Edit 1: Step 2 & LOOK-ALIKES (Trap A: $19\text{ u} \rightarrow 55\text{ u}$ Water-Ice Mimicry & Trap D: Type 2 Organics)
In **Step 2**, add the water-comb and organic-broadness check:
> **Step 2 - Salt-rich:** Na+ must dominate and at least one sodium salt cluster must be present. Water-ice, organic, mineral, Type 5 and noise spectra are non-3-OH (see LOOK-ALIKES). **Beware of the Type 1 water-ice trap: H3O+ (19 u) to H3O+(H2O)2 (55 u) has a time difference of 1.45 us, nearly identical to the 1.49 us spacing of Na+ (23 u) to Na+(NaOH) (63 u)! If there is a strong peak 0.80-0.85 us after the first main peak (H3O+(H2O) at 37 u) that is comparable to or taller than the +1.45 us peak, or a regular water comb (+0.82, +1.45, +1.98, +2.45 us), the first peak is H3O+ (19 u) and the spectrum is Type 1 water ice (non-3-OH).**

In **Step 3**, clarify peak sharpness against Type 2 organic humps:
> **Step 3 - Hydroxide backbone:** a strong, sharp, narrow Na+(NaOH) line 1.49 us after Na+ (allow about +-0.1 us), clearly above the noise and usually the second-highest peak. **If the feature near +1.4-1.6 us is a broad, rounded hump (spanning >0.35 us) accompanied by broad intermediate features (26-45 u) or wavy baseline oscillations in the second half of the record, it is Type 2 organic-bearing ice (non-3-OH).** Na+(NaOH)2, 2.53 us after Na+, is often visible and weaker. A weak or doubtful 63 u line means non-3-OH.

### Surgical Edit 2: Step 5 (Trap B: 3-Car Carbonate / 3-P Phosphate Detection on Log Scale)
In **Step 5**, specify the log-scale prominence criterion for $129\text{ u}$ and $85\text{ u}$ so moderate carbonate peaks are never dismissed as noise:
> **Step 5 - No carbonate, no phosphate:** no distinct line about 3.0-3.2 us after Na+ (129 u carbonate at +3.10 us or 125 u phosphate at +3.02 us), no Na+(Na2O) line 2.05-2.15 us after Na+ (85 u, which merges with the 81 u position), and no phosphate lines about 3.8 and 4.2 us after Na+. **On this log-scaled plot, the 129 u carbonate peak at +3.05 to +3.20 us after Na+ is often moderate in height (0.35-0.55): if a coherent peak rises distinctly (>=0.12 above its local baseline) at +3.00 to +3.20 us after Na+—especially when accompanied by any peak or shoulder at +2.00 to +2.15 us (85 u Na+(Na2O)) not explained by a taller 41 u hydrate line—classify as non-3-OH (3-Car). Do not dismiss a coherent +3.1 us peak as baseline noise.**

### Surgical Edit 3: Step 6 (Trap C: 3-KNa / 3-K Potassium Threshold & $95\text{ u}$ vs $103\text{ u}$ Spacing)
In **Step 6**, refine the $\text{K}^+$ log-scale threshold ($\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+ < 10$ means $\text{K}^+$ is within $1$ decade of $\text{Na}^+$) and distinguish $+2.34\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ from $+2.53\text{ }\mu\text{s}$:
> **Step 6 - Not potassium-rich:** the line 0.65-0.76 us after Na+ (K+ at 39 u merges with Na+(H2O) at 41 u) stays well below Na+, and there are no K+(KOH) (+2.34 us after Na+, 95 u) or K+(KCl) (+2.76 us after Na+, 113 u) lines. **Because the y-axis is logarithmic (where Na+/K+ < 10 corresponds to a small height difference), if a sharp K+ peak at +0.65 to +0.75 us after Na+ reaches >0.65 of the Na+ height (or rises sharply >=0.25 above the post-Na+ trough as a prominent peak comparable to the 63 u line, without a water/hydrate series), or if the second cluster peak sits at +2.30 to +2.40 us (K+(KOH) at 95 u) rather than +2.53 us (Na+(NaOH)2 at 103 u), the grain is potassium-rich (3-KNa or 3-K) -> non-3-OH.**
